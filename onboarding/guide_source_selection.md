# Onboarding Guide: Source Selection

This guide helps you choose books and sources for your NotebookLM notebooks.
The source library (`templates/source_library.yaml`) contains curated
recommendations organized by clinical topic. This guide explains the selection
logic so you can make informed choices — or deviate from the recommendations
when you have good reasons.

---

## How the Recommendations Work

The onboarding wizard maps your clinical landscape (what brings you to this
work) to a set of recommended notebooks, then recommends specific sources for
each notebook from the source library.

Sources are marked as:
- **Core** — foundational texts for that topic; include at least 2-3 of these
- **Supplemental** — add depth; include if the topic is a primary focus

The wizard explains why each source is recommended. Don't add books you don't
understand the rationale for — a mismatch between your situation and a source's
framing can produce unhelpful responses.

## Core Notebooks: Recommended Sources

### Core Therapy Techniques

These are the foundations. Everyone should have this notebook.

**Core sources:**
- *The Happiness Trap* — Russ Harris
  Written for general readers, not therapists. Explains ACT without jargon.
  If you can only have one ACT book, this is it.
- *No Bad Parts* — Richard Schwartz
  IFS for a general audience. Accessible, practical, and the model Stillpoint
  uses for parts work. Essential.
- *Get Out of Your Mind and Into Your Life* — Steven Hayes
  ACT workbook. More clinical than Harris but with practical exercises. Good
  if you want to go deeper on ACT.

**Supplemental:**
- *Mindfulness in Plain English* — Bhante Gunaratana
  Foundational meditation instruction, not clinical but essential background.
- *The ACT Practitioner's Guide* — various
  More technical. Add this only if you want technical ACT depth.

### Self-Compassion & Shame Resilience

Required. Shame shows up in every presenting concern — often before anything
else surfaces.

**Core sources:**
- *Self-Compassion: The Proven Power of Being Kind to Yourself* — Kristin Neff
  The foundational research text on self-compassion. Practical and accessible.
- *I Thought It Was Just Me (But It Isn't)* — Brené Brown
  Shame resilience for general readers. The framework is cited throughout
  Stillpoint's session protocols.
- *The Mindful Path to Self-Compassion* — Christopher Germer
  Clinical, but readable. Pairs well with Neff.

**Supplemental:**
- *Fierce Self-Compassion* — Kristin Neff
  More recent, focused on boundaries and fierce (not just soft) self-compassion.
- *Daring Greatly* — Brené Brown
  More on vulnerability and shame in relationships; add if relational dynamics
  are a primary focus.

## Tailored Notebooks: Key Sources by Topic

### CPTSD & Trauma

- *The Body Keeps the Score* — Bessel van der Kolk — trauma's somatic dimension
- *Complex PTSD: From Surviving to Thriving* — Pete Walker — practical and direct,
  especially strong on emotional flashbacks and the inner critic
- *Healing the Fragmented Selves of Trauma Survivors* — Janina Fisher — IFS +
  trauma; more clinical but excellent for parts work in trauma context
- *What My Bones Know* — Stephanie Foo — memoir; useful for recognition and
  normalization; not clinical but often powerful

Note: Do not add a Trauma notebook unless trauma is actually a primary
presenting concern. Adding it without grounding creates a risk the system
over-pathologizes ordinary distress as trauma.

### ADHD & Executive Function

- *Driven to Distraction* — Hallowell & Ratey — foundational; explains the ADHD
  experience rather than managing it (important for self-compassion)
- *ADHD 2.0* — Hallowell & Ratey — updated with newer research
- *Your Brain's Not Broken* — Tamara Rosier — emotional dysregulation in ADHD,
  practical
- *Smart but Stuck* — Thomas Brown — ADHD and executive function without
  hyperactivity; essential if inattentive presentation

### ASD & Neurodivergence

- *Unmasking Autism* — Devon Price — late-diagnosed autism, masking, identity
- *I Think I Might Be Autistic* — Cynthia Kim — accessible introduction
- *The Autistic Brain* — Temple Grandin — sensory and cognitive differences
- *Neurotribes* — Steve Silberman — history and context; good for
  de-pathologizing and understanding the neurodiversity frame

### Anxiety & OCD

- *Dare* — Barry McDonagh — ACT-adjacent anxiety approach; practical
- *The Anxiety and Phobia Workbook* — Edmund Bourne — comprehensive CBT approach
- *Freedom from Obsessive Compulsive Disorder* — Jonathan Grayson — OCD
  specifically; do not add if OCD is not a presenting concern
- *Anxious* — Joseph LeDoux — neuroscience of anxiety; more academic

### Depression & Mood

- *The Mindful Way Through Depression* — Williams, Teasdale, Segal, Kabat-Zinn
  MBCT (mindfulness-based cognitive therapy); evidence-based
- *Feeling Good* — David Burns — classic CBT; practical and direct
- *Lost Connections* — Johann Hari — social/systemic causes of depression;
  useful for de-medicalizing and finding meaning; not clinical
- *The Depression Cure* — Stephen Ilardi — lifestyle factors; practical

### Substance Use & Recovery

- *In the Realm of Hungry Ghosts* — Gabor Maté — trauma-informed addiction model;
  foundational for compassionate framing
- *This Naked Mind* — Annie Grace — focused on alcohol; ACT-adjacent
- *Quit Like a Woman* — Holly Whitaker — alcohol, feminist lens
- *Recovery: Freedom from Our Addictions* — Russell Brand — memoir, 12-step
  informed but accessible

### Compulsive Sexual Behavior

- *Out of the Shadows* — Patrick Carnes — foundational; clinical
- *Facing the Shadow* — Patrick Carnes — workbook companion
- *Love You Hate the Porn* — Mark Chamberlain — relationship impact
- *Don't Call It Love* — Patrick Carnes — trauma and compulsive sexuality

Note: This is a shame-saturated area. The Self-Compassion notebook must be set
up and working before sessions in this domain are productive.

### Grief & Loss

- *On Grief and Grieving* — Kübler-Ross & Kessler — foundational; normalizes
  the grief experience
- *Finding Meaning* — David Kessler — the "sixth stage"; building a life after loss
- *The Year of Magical Thinking* — Joan Didion — memoir; powerful for recognition
- *It's OK That You're Not OK* — Megan Devine — non-pathologizing, grief-positive

### Relationship & Attachment

- *Attached* — Levine & Heller — attachment styles for general readers; popular
  and accessible
- *Hold Me Tight* — Sue Johnson — EFT for couples; works for relationship
  patterns even outside couples context
- *Adult Children of Emotionally Immature Parents* — Lindsay Gibson — relevant
  for relational patterns with family of origin
- *The Dance of Anger* — Harriet Lerner — boundaries, relationship dynamics

### Anger & Emotional Regulation

- *The Anger Trap* — Les Carter — practical, cognitive approach
- *When Anger Hurts* — McKay, Rogers, McKay — CBT approach to anger management
- *Rage Becomes Her* — Soraya Chemaly — feminist framing of suppressed anger;
  normalizing and validating before regulation
- *DBT Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets* — Marsha Linehan — clinical
  workbook; add only if DBT is the chosen modality

### Body Image & Eating

- *Intuitive Eating* — Tribole & Resch — evidence-based, anti-diet
- *Health at Every Size* — Lindo Bacon — weight-neutral, anti-shame
- *8 Keys to Recovery from an Eating Disorder* — Carolyn Costin — recovery
  focused; includes memoir elements
- *The Body Is Not an Apology* — Sonya Renee Taylor — radical body acceptance

### Digital Addiction

- *Irresistible* — Adam Alter — behavioral addiction, design manipulation
- *Digital Minimalism* — Cal Newport — practical; values-based approach
- *Hooked* — Nir Eyal — how apps are designed to hook you (important for
  understanding the mechanism, not a recovery book)
- *Indistractable* — Nir Eyal — more practical; managing distraction

### Sleep & Chronic Health

- *Why We Sleep* — Matthew Walker — sleep science; foundational
- *The Sleep Solution* — W. Chris Winter — CBT-I approach, practical
- *How to Be Sick* — Toni Bernhard — chronic illness and self-compassion;
  Buddhist-informed
- *The Spoon Theory* — Christine Miserandino — energy management for chronic illness

### Identity & Life Transitions

- *Transitions* — William Bridges — framework for navigating change
- *Man's Search for Meaning* — Viktor Frankl — meaning-making; foundational
- *The Second Mountain* — David Brooks — purpose and recommitment after midlife
- *Untamed* — Glennon Doyle — memoir; identity reclamation

## How to Acquire Sources

**PDF (preferred for NotebookLM):**
- Purchase from publishers or Amazon Kindle → export to PDF
- Library digital lending: Libby / OverDrive → borrow ePub → convert to PDF
- Internet Archive lending library (one-at-a-time borrowing)

**Google Drive:**
If you have Google Workspace, upload PDFs to Drive and link them directly
into NotebookLM — it stays up to date automatically.

**Pasting text:**
For shorter documents (articles, excerpts), NotebookLM accepts pasted text.
Not recommended as a primary source format.

## What to Avoid

- **Pop psychology without clinical grounding** — Books that make strong
  therapeutic claims without research basis can produce confident but
  misleading responses.
- **Sources that contradict your modality** — If your approach is ACT-based,
  adding a strictly Freudian text creates framework conflicts.
- **Outdated clinical texts** — The DSM-IV is not the DSM-5-TR. Old editions
  can produce outdated diagnostic language.
- **Workbooks without reference texts** — Workbooks are great supplemental
  sources but work best paired with the corresponding full book.
